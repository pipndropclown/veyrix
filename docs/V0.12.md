# Veyrix V0.12 — Research Cache & Reproducible Validation

Veyrix remains paper-trading and historical simulation only. It never connects wallets, moves funds, submits orders or transactions, uses leverage, or creates short positions. Historical results do not predict future performance.

## Research identity and versions

A canonical research configuration records the strategy set, timeframe, normalized UTC end anchor, validation method, parameter-grid version, execution costs, risk and sizing rules, embargo and fold sizes, Coinbase source/granularity, and explicit strategy, execution, validation, regime, score, grid, and cache-schema versions. Object keys are recursively sorted before serialization.

`researchRunId = "VRX-" + first 24 hexadecimal characters of SHA-256({ canonical configuration, dataset fingerprint })`

Equivalent inputs produce the same ID. Any material configuration, version, anchor, or normalized dataset change produces another ID, preventing stale logic or revised candles from being treated as equivalent.

## Cache architecture

Research results use a separate cache from the 15-minute historical-data cache. The research cache is keyed by `researchRunId`, uses a 12-hour TTL, validates the configuration and `veyrix-cache-v1` schema before reuse, and stores only complete results. Production uses a process-local in-memory best-effort cache. Development layers an optional `.veyrix-cache` filesystem store beneath memory. The interface is deliberately store-agnostic so a durable zero/low-cost store can replace it later.

Serverless memory and ephemeral files are not durable: deployments, instance recycling, scaling, and eviction can remove entries. V0.12 does not claim otherwise.

An in-flight promise registry is keyed by run ID. Simultaneous identical uncached jobs share one analysis promise. The entry is removed in `finally`; failed jobs are never cached and do not remove older unrelated valid records.

Responses expose `MISS`, `HIT`, or `IN_FLIGHT_REUSED`, cached age, the run ID, and recorded timings.

## Fingerprints and manifest

The dataset fingerprint is SHA-256 over chronologically sorted normalized `timestamp`, `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, and `volume` fields. The result fingerprint is SHA-256 over a recursively key-sorted result structure. These fingerprints detect changed inputs and accidental result mutation; they are debugging/integrity aids, not cryptographic proof of trading correctness.

The `veyrix-research-v1` manifest includes run ID, generation time, historical boundaries, six-hour interval, candle count and source, data quality, strategies and parameter grids, fee/slippage and collision assumptions, risk and sizing settings, validation method and fold settings, component versions, full/matched benchmark allocations, and dataset fingerprint.

Exports contain the cache schema version, manifest, configuration, summaries and complete strategy/validation/benchmark results, research scores, timings, and fingerprints. Filenames are deterministic. Import preparation validates supported schema, manifest/run-ID consistency, and the result fingerprint; V0.12 does not execute imported code or strategies.

## Anchored and multi-anchor research

Research dates are parsed and normalized to the preceding six-hour UTC boundary. Malformed dates, future dates, dates before Coinbase SOL history support, and ranges crossing the configured provider floor are rejected. Retrieval is bounded by the explicit anchor, so no later candle may enter the dataset.

Multi-anchor validation uses exactly three chronological end anchors: latest selected anchor minus 120 days, minus 60 days, and the selected anchor. Each anchor runs the identical 180D or 365D all-strategy extended-validation methodology. Runs execute sequentially to bound memory and provider load; cached sub-runs are reused. Historical pagination remains sequential, at most 300 candles per Coinbase request: 180D uses about 721 candles/3 requests and 365D about 1,461 candles/5 requests.

## Temporal metrics

Anchor Consistency is experimental and bounded 0–100:

- 30% rank similarity: `clamp(100 - 50 × rank range)`
- 25% OOS direction consistency: `100` when all aggregate OOS returns share a sign, otherwise `40`
- 25% robustness similarity: `clamp(100 - 2 × robustness-score range)`
- 20% research-score similarity: `clamp(100 - 2 × research-score range)`

Temporal Reliability:

- `INSUFFICIENT DATA`: fewer than two anchors
- `STABLE`: consistency at least 75, positive-anchor rate at least 60%, robustness range at most 15, and matched-beat-rate range at most 30
- `VARIABLE`: consistency at least 45 and robustness range at most 30
- `UNSTABLE`: otherwise

Temporal Research Score is experimental and bounded 0–100:

`35% average Research Score + 30% Anchor Consistency + 20% average Multi-Regime Robustness + 15% positive-anchor rate`

It ranks the quality and temporal consistency of the simulated evidence; it is not a profitability guarantee.

## Performance instrumentation

Records include historical retrieval, normalization, parameter sensitivity, validation-method, regime-analysis, strategy-arena, aggregate analysis, and total durations. The current extended-validation engine executes as one synchronous arena, so its inclusive arena duration is recorded for the nested phase fields; consumers must not sum those inclusive values. No telemetry, user tracking, or personal analytics is added.

## Performance and safety

Normalized candles are frozen and reused across every strategy and fold. Existing bounded parameter grids remain unchanged. A multi-anchor run is capped at three sequential anchors, uses cached sub-analyses, and never fetches separately per strategy or fold. Research code has no access to live portfolio persistence, processed IDs, daily-loss state, or live strategy configuration.

## Known limitations

Serverless caching is best effort, not durable or shared across every instance. The first 365D multi-anchor run remains computationally expensive. Coinbase candles can be revised or incomplete, causing a deliberate new run identity. Six-hour OHLC cannot reveal exact intrabar paths. Three overlapping historical anchor ranges are not independent samples. Parameter optimization can overfit, and execution omits variable liquidity, order-book depth, latency, market impact, partial fills, taxes, and changing fee tiers.
