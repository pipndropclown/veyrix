# Veyrix V0.10 — Multi-Regime Walk-Forward Analysis

Veyrix remains paper-trading and historical simulation only. It does not connect wallets, move funds, submit exchange or blockchain orders, use leverage or margin, or create short positions. Historical results do not predict future performance.

## Historical retrieval and pagination

Coinbase Exchange remains the public source for SOL-USD OHLCV candles. Retrieval happens only in the server-side historical service and remains cached by timeframe for 15 minutes.

| Timeframe | Coinbase granularity | Expected candles | Provider requests |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 30D | 21,600 seconds / 6 hours | 121 | 1 |
| 60D | 21,600 seconds / 6 hours | 241 | 1 |
| 90D | 21,600 seconds / 6 hours | 361 | 2 |

Each request spans at most 299 candle intervals, which yields at most 300 candles including both boundaries. Requests execute sequentially. Adjacent ranges intentionally share one boundary timestamp; normalization validates OHLC relationships, removes malformed records and duplicate timestamps, and sorts the merged result chronologically. The complete timeframe is fetched once and rolling windows are built in memory—there is no request per window.

## Rolling-window methodology

- Analysis window: 30 days / 121 six-hour candles.
- Step: 10 days / 40 candles.
- 60D history produces 4 overlapping windows.
- 90D history produces 7 overlapping windows.
- Every window uses the existing chronological 70% in-sample and 30% out-of-sample split: 84 IS and 37 OOS candles.
- The existing V0.9 sensitivity grid is evaluated only on that window's IS segment.
- The highest in-sample experimental Veyrix Score configuration is frozen and evaluated only on the OOS segment.

All windows use the same $10,000 balance, 10% position sizing, 2% stop, 4% target, 0.10% fee per side, 0.05% adverse deterministic slippage, long-only restriction, and `STOP_FIRST` collision policy. Both full-exposure SOL and 10%-exposure SOL benchmarks are calculated on each OOS segment with the same costs.

## Market regime classifier

The classifier is deterministic and not machine learning. It calculates full-window start-to-end return, population standard deviation of six-hour close-to-close percentage returns, and the final close's percentage deviation from the window average.

Rules are evaluated in this order:

1. `HIGH_VOLATILITY` when realized six-hour volatility is at least 2.50%.
2. `BULLISH` when window return is at least +5.00%.
3. `BEARISH` when window return is at most -5.00%.
4. `SIDEWAYS` otherwise.
5. `UNAVAILABLE` for fewer than two valid candles or non-finite metrics.

The trend-deviation metric is reported for inspection but does not override these thresholds.

## Cross-window analytics and consistency

Cross-window reporting includes profitable and losing OOS windows, win-window rate, average/median/best/worst OOS return, average OOS Veyrix Score, average robustness and stability, high-overfitting count, maximum OOS drawdown, population standard deviation of returns, and full/matched benchmark beat rates.

The experimental Cross-Window Consistency score is bounded 0–100:

- `Variability = clamp(100 - 20 × OOS return standard deviation, 0, 100)`
- `Robustness stability = clamp(100 - 2 × robustness-score standard deviation, 0, 100)`
- A severe negative window has OOS return at or below -2 percentage points.
- `Severe protection = 100 - severe-negative-window percentage`
- `Consistency = 40% profitable-window rate + 25% variability + 20% robustness stability + 15% severe protection`

## Regime performance and multi-regime score

For every regime, Veyrix reports window count, average OOS return, profitable-window percentage, and average robustness. A regime is acceptable when it has at least one window, non-negative average OOS return, and at least 50% profitable windows. Missing regimes are not fabricated and do not count as acceptable.

The experimental Multi-Regime Robustness Score is bounded 0–100:

- 35% average V0.9 robustness score.
- 30% Cross-Window Consistency.
- 20% acceptable regimes divided by all four defined regimes.
- 15% matched-benchmark beat rate.

## Reliability labels

- `STRONG`: score at least 75, profitable OOS windows at least 60%, and high-overfitting windows no more than 20%.
- `PROMISING`: score at least 60, profitable OOS windows at least 50%, and high-overfitting windows no more than 40%.
- `MIXED`: score at least 40.
- `WEAK`: score below 40.
- `UNAVAILABLE`: required metrics are missing.

These are descriptive experimental labels, not predictions or statistical guarantees.

## Parameter drift and temporal stability

Selected parameter objects are canonicalized by sorted key/value pairs. Veyrix reports the modal configuration, number of unique configurations, modal-use percentage, and changes between consecutive windows.

For each transition, every parameter change is divided by that parameter's full configured range. The mean normalized change across that strategy's parameters is converted to similarity. Temporal Parameter Stability is the average transition similarity multiplied by 100 and clamped to 0–100. This per-parameter averaging prevents a strategy from receiving an advantage merely because it has fewer parameters.

- `LOW` drift: temporal stability at least 75 and configurations change in no more than 50% of transitions.
- `HIGH` drift: temporal stability below 45 or configurations change in more than 75% of transitions.
- `MODERATE`: all other valid cases.
- `UNAVAILABLE`: no selected configurations.

## Isolation and limitations

Multi-regime analysis works only with immutable historical inputs and isolated backtest portfolios. It does not import or mutate live persistence, localStorage, daily-loss state, processed evaluation IDs, live trades, or live strategy configuration.

Rolling windows overlap and therefore are not independent statistical samples. Sixty or ninety days can still omit important regimes. Regime thresholds and reliability scores are heuristics. Parameter optimization can still overfit. Six-hour OHLC candles obscure intrabar paths, and simulations omit variable spreads, liquidity, order-book depth, latency, market impact, partial fills, taxes, and changing exchange fees.
